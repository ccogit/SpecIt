# Discovery and requirements elicitation

Adapted for SpecIt's multiple-choice interview from the open-source **requirements-analysis** skill (Emmraan, MIT). The original skill is a broader requirements-analysis process; this focused adaptation does not claim to be its unmodified distribution.

## Apply
- Establish the problem, intended outcome, primary users, decision maker, product stage, in-scope and out-of-scope boundaries before selecting implementation details.
- Identify the primary workflow, business rules, exceptions, permission boundaries, data lifetime, integration points and measurable quality constraints.
- Ask only consequential unknowns. Do not re-ask settled decisions, turn guesses into requirements, or recommend a technology without a stated constraint.
- Cover the six SpecIt topics: Ziel, Nutzer, Funktionen, UX, Technik, Qualität. Use concise, mutually exclusive options unless multi-select is genuinely needed.
- Propose safe, explicit defaults with a reason; a default is an assumption, not a confirmed decision.
- Follow-up questions target gaps, conflicts and downstream choices generated by earlier answers. Prioritize blockers over cosmetic preferences.
- Do not demand personal or confidential data to complete the interview.

## Consistency gate
Preserve the terminology in the user's idea. Distinguish confirmed choices, assumed defaults and unresolved questions. If two choices conflict, ask for a resolution instead of silently selecting one.
